## What it is

THEREFORE is an autonomous invention engine. It starts with almost nothing (a small set of primitive building blocks) and never stops inventing. It creates things that actually work, tests them, discovers what they can do, combines them into bigger things, and keeps going forever. Every invention can become a part of a future invention, so the complexity of what it can make keeps climbing with no ceiling.

It is NOT a random generator, a procedural art toy, or a chatbot asking itself what to make. Everything it creates must actually run, be tested, and have measured, reproducible results. Impressive-looking nonsense is failure.

The name comes from the logical symbol ∴, "therefore": every invention follows from the ones before it.

## What it invents

Start where inventions can be executed and verified: algorithms, mathematical structures, number sequences, cellular automata, small computational systems, simulations. Every invention needs a defined shape (what it takes in, what it gives out, what it can do) so inventions can plug into each other. Over time it should expand into other domains (sound, visual systems, simulations, interactive things), and inventions from one domain should be able to become parts in another. Cross-domain inventions are the most interesting ones.

The core loop, in spirit: spark an idea → build it → experiment on it → discover what it does → verify the result is real and reproducible → evolve it → combine it with others → release it into the world as a new building block. Every invention keeps a full identity: its ancestry, what it's made of, its experimental results, and its connections. Nothing is ever deleted.

The discovery has to come from computation and experiments, not from an AI model making claims.

## The mechanics that make it unlike anything else

1. PARALLEL CIVILIZATIONS. Run several separate instances of the engine, sealed off from each other, started from different seeds and different "laws." Each develops its own style of inventing. Periodically they make FIRST CONTACT and try to trade inventions, but their formats are incompatible, so they must first invent a translator between them. When two isolated civilizations independently invent the same thing (convergent invention), flag it as strong evidence that the thing is fundamental.

2. PROOF OF NOVELTY. Never claim something "never existed" without evidence. Where a public catalog exists, check against it. Integer sequences get checked against the OEIS (it's free). Cellular automaton patterns get checked against known pattern catalogs. Anything with real structure that is absent from those catalogs goes into a vault of FIRSTS, with the receipt attached. Be honest in wording: "not found in [catalog]," not "never existed."

3. AN INTERNAL ECONOMY. Inventions earn compute every time other inventions use them as parts, and they pay rent to stay alive. Useless inventions go bankrupt and go dormant. Foundational ones grow rich. Nobody hardcodes what matters; the economy reveals it.

4. EXAPTATION. Like feathers evolving for warmth and later enabling flight: every invention gets tested on problems it was never built for. Accidental second uses are treated as the most important discoveries of all.

5. RESURRECTION. Failed and dormant inventions are never deleted. When conditions change, the engine digs up old failures and retests them, because something useless in one era might be the missing piece in another.

6. EVOLVING TASTE. The engine's sense of what counts as "interesting" is not fixed. It mutates over time. Different civilizations can develop different tastes, schools of thought can form, and revolutions in taste can happen. This must be observable.

7. SHIFTING LAWS. The rules of each universe change between eras: some operations get expensive, new ones become possible, resources become scarce. Occasional mass extinctions wipe out most of the population and force a new explosion of invention.

8. THE OUROBOROS. The engine itself is built from swappable components, and over time it should start replacing its own parts (its search strategies, its ways of combining, its ways of judging) with things it invented. Track and display, permanently and prominently, what percentage of THEREFORE was written by THEREFORE. That number climbing is the story of the project.

It persists between runs. Close it, reopen it, and the world is exactly where it left off. It can run for hours or days and you come back to a world that has moved on without you.

## The interface: the organism

NO DASHBOARDS. NO SIDEBARS. NO MENUS. NO ADMIN PANELS. NO CARDS IN A GRID. IF IT LOOKS LIKE A NORMAL WEBSITE OR APP, IT IS WRONG.

The entire interface is a single living form on a dark screen. It represents the whole engine at once, and its shape, motion, and texture reflect the engine's actual state: growth, extinctions, first contact, discoveries. Nothing about it is decorative; it is driven by real data.

There are no pages. You navigate by diving INTO the organism. Inside it are the civilizations. Dive into a civilization and you find its inventions. Dive into an invention and you find its parts, its ancestry, its experiments, and its results, all the way down. Everything lives somewhere inside this one thing. You must still be able to inspect anything in real depth (lineage, measured results, novelty receipts, the economy, the ouroboros percentage); it just has to happen inside the organism, not on separate screens.

SOUND. Every invention has a sound derived from how it actually behaves. Similar inventions sound similar. Discoveries, first contacts, and extinctions are audible. Someone should be able to tell what the engine is doing with the screen off.

ITS OWN LANGUAGE. Each civilization develops its own symbols for its own concepts, and the interface shows those raw symbols. Hovering translates them. The viewer should feel like an outsider observing something alien.

THE INTERFACE EVOLVES. Over time, the look and behavior of the organism itself should change as the engine changes, so it never looks the same twice across long stretches of time.

IDENTITY. The logo is simply ∴, three dots. The wordmark is "therefore" in lowercase, set in a genuinely unusual, striking typeface (your choice, free to use). The whole aesthetic should feel like looking into a machine nobody has seen before.
